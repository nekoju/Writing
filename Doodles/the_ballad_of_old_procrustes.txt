The Ballad of Old Procrustes


The bandit he stretched all the visitors to length
Before tying them tight to his bed
And the taller were hacked and all mangled, belovedly
Lashed to the posts so they'd fit
But for all of the protests through all of the pain
Old Procrustes could hardly but wince
As he had what he needed, a guest in his home
Who for leaving, could hardly but beg

The butcher, the baker, the painter, and minstrel
All came bearing gifts to the door
And old lonely Procrustes couldn't but measure
The soon-to-be spot on the floor
Where the limbs and the lashes would surely soon pile
At the bedside of now-esteemed guests
As he sawed and he panted and tightened and granted
All the growth of the undertall best

And lonely Procrustes, he spoke and regaled
All his visitors' yearnings for tales
Of good, bad, and middlin, aloud from the kitchen
While preparing a paella of snails
"Try it; the Greeks and the Hebrews all like it!"
Fed the painter a-lacking for limbs
And suppressing the gag for the moment itself
He cried out that the food wasn't his

So I went by, unwitting of all of the stories
Of people too big made to fit
And though kept in good comfort with tea and with crumpets
And Procrustes he hacked me to bits
And now that I'm speaking and warning the would-be
weary hostelry seekers too tall
Be wary each time that you knock on the door
'Fore the pieces that make you to fall
