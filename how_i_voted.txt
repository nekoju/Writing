How I voted


It might come as a surprise to some that read this, but I voted for Kamala Harris. Why? Well the reasoning goes like this.
  - I live in Indiana, a thoroughly red state
  - Therefore, my presidential vote is extantly meaningless
  - My leftist friends will still talk to me if I vote
  - My liberal friends will not if I don't
  - I should preserve my mental health, community, sense of belonging, whatever. I should consider giving the benefit of the doubt to the incrementalists to stave off the growth of fascism and the untimely demise or further suffering of me and people I care about. But ultimately it doesn't matter, so the only opportunity cost is pride, which I should just swallow for the greater good or something.
Expert at fawning that I am, decided that simply succumbing to the emotional blackmail of the blue-no-matter-who crowd was the least worst option, both for me and *very possibly* others. I'd be able to say "well actually I did vote, but we should still be having these conversations". I believed that fawning would win my dignity.

Maybe it was the right choice or wasn't. I won't ever know because time doesn't work that way.

But I *do* know thoroughly that whatever curiosity I engaged this system in was not and will not be reciprocated by the loudest of those voices, proclaiming the way forward is backward into willfull ignorance about imperialism and genocide and the way money and power work. Who can't even consider the reality that the empire itself is the problem. Who root for botoxed politicians like they root for a sports team, itself likely the hobby project of a billionaire, while disparaging people far more materially affected than them for opting out and refusing complicity in the demise of their own families overseas. Who nod agreeably at talking points about the expansion of the surveillance state under sufficiently polite murderers.

At the same time I enjoy no real political communion with those values I most align with, having largely felt dismissed for being overly curious, or about the wrong things. Or more generally that the materialists I knew didn't really know a whole lot about how science works or how to use it. I've explicitly been told to stay out of organizing, with a greatest-hits list of hypothetical insults that has become instantly recognizable and always begins with some variant of "the CIA couldn't make a better wrecker than you." The upshot is that I basically just stay depressed and make my stupid little art and pray to Muller this generation's tankies are more discerning than those that we've been propagandized to believe existed in generations past, and mutter "that guy sure was an asshole," and carry on.

Liberals don't have to be "right", but all of this is doomed if we can't learn to empathize with optimists. Make no mistake; I firmly believe that things must get worse before they can get better. We would like costless progress; and all of the lies of capitalism (which are not lies but programs) are of thermodynamics.
